The benchmark: one week of English Bluesky (Sep 18–25, 2026). Everything the held-out accounts did that week (posts, replies, likes, follows, reposts) and real news-account posts are copied into the simulation at the real moment; the rest of the network is simulated. This is run \(K = 8\) times with different seeds, giving 8 futures. Both losses are proper scores: the best a simulator can do is report its true uncertainty. Lower is better; 0 is perfect; neither is capped.

Loss 1 — Liker Brier (likes)

For each held-out post, did each person like it, and how sure was the simulation? Scored once per post at the end of the week.

\[ L_1 \;=\; \operatorname*{mean}_{g \in \text{groups}} \;\operatorname*{mean}_{a \in g} \;\operatorname*{mean}_{q \in \text{posts}(a)} \; \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{u \in U_q} \big(p_{u,q} - y_{u,q}\big)^2}{\max(L_q,\,1)} \]
  • \(q\) one held-out post (replies count as posts).
  • \(U_q\) everyone who liked \(q\) by the end of the week in reality or in at least one of the 8 futures. Anyone else would contribute exactly 0, so no candidate list is needed.
  • \(p_{u,q}\) \(= \tfrac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{1}[\,u \text{ liked } q \text{ in future } k\,]\): the share of futures in which person \(u\) liked the post. The simulation's probability, in eighths.
  • \(y_{u,q}\) 1 if \(u\) really liked \(q\), else 0.
  • \((p-y)^2\) the Brier score for that person. Squared, not plain distance: plain distance is minimised by rounding every person to 0 or 1 (a majority vote across futures), which throws away the uncertainty the futures were run to capture. Squaring charges a full 1 for confidently wrong and only 0.09 for saying 30% to someone who didn't like it, so the best move is the true probability. Splits into misses \(\sum_{y=1}(1-p)^2\) and false alarms \(\sum_{y=0}p^2\).
  • \(L_q\) the post's real like count. Dividing by it turns the sum into "share of this post's likes gotten wrong, with partial credit": 0 = perfect, 1 = produced nothing, 2 = right count from all the wrong people. Without it, one 2,000-like post would outweigh 400 five-like posts. \(\max(L_q,1)\) only avoids dividing by zero.
  • means post → account → sampling group → overall, each a plain mean. Every group weighs the same, so the growth accounts count as much as everyone else.

Loss 2 — Follower CRPS (followers)

For each held-out account and each day, how many followers it gained and lost, split by the size of the follower. Which exact person followed is not scored (at under 1% per person, 8 futures can't resolve it); what kind of account followed is.

\[ L_2 \;=\; \operatorname*{mean}_{g \in \text{groups}} \;\operatorname*{mean}_{a \in g} \;\operatorname*{mean}_{d=1}^{7} \; \sum_{s \in S} \operatorname{CRPS}\!\big(x^{(s)}_{a,d},\; y^{(s)}_{a,d}\big) \]
\[ \operatorname{CRPS}(x, y) \;=\; \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \big|x_k - y\big| \;-\; \frac{1}{2K(K-1)} \sum_{k \ne j} \big|x_k - x_j\big| \]
  • \(S\) six series per account per day: new followers from accounts with under 1K followers, 1K–10K, over 10K, and unfollows in the same three bands. Size is read at the start of the week, the same for real and simulated. Total gain and net change are sums of these and are not scored separately.
  • \(y\) \(= \log(1 + \text{real count})\) for that series, account and day.
  • \(x_k\) \(= \log(1 + \text{simulated count in future } k)\), \(k = 1..8\). Log first, then compare, so a distance of 0.69 means "off by 2×" whether the day had 10 followers or 1,000. Percent change would be lopsided and would cap at 1; log keeps counting.
  • first term average distance of the futures from reality.
  • second term half the average distance between futures, the credit for spread. Without it the score is minimised by collapsing all 8 futures onto one value; with the full spread subtracted a scattered forecast could score below zero. Half is the amount that makes a forecast shaped like reality the best one. When all 8 futures agree, CRPS is plain \(|x - y|\).
  • \(K(K-1)\) pairs over \(k \ne j\), the "fair" form: unbiased at any number of futures, so 8 and 32 are judged alike.
  • means day → account → group → overall. The number stays in log units: 0.69 ≈ off by 2×, 2.3 ≈ off by 10×.

Bars on the leaderboard

Bar height is \(e^{-\text{loss}}\): 1.0 for a perfect score, 0.5 at a loss of 0.69, 0.25 at 1.39. The list is ordered by the combined score \(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(e^{-L_1} + e^{-L_2}\big)\), higher first.
