All submissions, best first

One week of English Bluesky, Sep 18–25, 2026 (days 1–7 are UTC days, day 1 = Sep 18). Everything the held-out accounts did that week and real news-account posts are copied into the simulation at the real moment; the rest of the network is simulated, \(K = 8\) times with different seeds. Three losses: who liked each post (Loss 1), how many followers each account gained and lost (Loss 2), and how many likes each post got (Loss 3). Each is reported at its headline timescale and again on 2-day windows, so every entry has six numbers. Lower is better, 0 is perfect, none is capped.

Loss 1 — Liker Brier (likes)

For every held-out post: did each person like it, and how sure was the simulation? Scored once per post at the end of the week.

per person
\( \displaystyle p_{u,q} \;=\; \dfrac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \mathbf{1}\big[\,u \text{ liked } q \text{ in future } k\,\big] \)
per post
\( \displaystyle S_q \;=\; \dfrac{1}{\max(L_q,\,1)} \sum_{u \in U_q} \big(p_{u,q} - y_{u,q}\big)^2 \)
overall
\( \displaystyle L_1 \;=\; \dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G}\; \dfrac{1}{|A_g|}\sum_{a \in A_g}\; \dfrac{1}{|Q_a|}\sum_{q \in Q_a} S_q \)
\(q\)
a held-out post (replies count too); \(Q_a\) the posts of account \(a\)
\(U_q\)
everyone who liked \(q\) in reality or in at least one future. Anyone else contributes exactly 0
\(p_{u,q}\)
share of the 8 futures in which person \(u\) liked \(q\): the simulation's probability, in eighths
\(y_{u,q}\)
1 if \(u\) really liked \(q\), else 0
\((p-y)^2\)
Brier score for that person. Misses \((1-p)^2\) over real likers, false alarms \(p^2\) over the rest
\(L_q\)
the post's real like count; \(\max(L_q,1)\) only avoids dividing by zero
\(G,\;A_g\)
the sampling groups and the held-out accounts in each; every group weighs the same

Reading it: 0 = every liker right in every future · 1 = produced no likes at all · 2 = right count, all the wrong people.

Why squared: plain distance \(|p-y|\) is minimised by rounding every person to 0 or 1, which throws away the uncertainty the futures exist to capture. Squaring charges a full 1 for confidently wrong and 0.09 for saying 30% to someone who didn't like it, so the best report is the true chance. Why divide by \(L_q\): otherwise one 2,000-like post outweighs 400 five-like posts. Dividing by reality only (never by anything the simulation controls) keeps it ungameable.

Loss 2 — Follower CRPS (followers)

For every held-out account and day: how many followers it gained and lost, split by the follower's size. The exact person is not scored (under 1% per person, 8 futures can't see it); the kind of account is.

log counts
\( \displaystyle y \;=\; \log(1 + \text{real count}) \qquad x_k \;=\; \log(1 + \text{count in future } k) \)
per series
\( \displaystyle \operatorname{CRPS}(x, y) \;=\; \dfrac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} |x_k - y| \;-\; \dfrac{1}{2K(K-1)} \sum_{k \neq j} |x_k - x_j| \)
per day
\( \displaystyle D_{a,d} \;=\; \sum_{s \in S} \operatorname{CRPS}\big(x^{(s)}_{a,d},\, y^{(s)}_{a,d}\big) \)
overall
\( \displaystyle L_2 \;=\; \dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G}\; \dfrac{1}{|A_g|}\sum_{a \in A_g}\; \dfrac{1}{7}\sum_{d=1}^{7} D_{a,d} \)
\(S\)
six series per account per day: new followers from accounts with <1K, 1K–10K, >10K followers, and unfollows in the same three bands (size read at the start of the week)
\(y,\;x_k\)
real and simulated counts on a log scale, so a distance of 0.69 means "off by 2×" whether the day had 10 followers or 1,000
first term
average distance of the 8 futures from reality
second term
half the average distance between futures: the credit for honest spread
\(K(K-1)\)
pairs over \(k \neq j\), the "fair" form, unbiased at any number of futures
\(d\)
day of the week, 1–7; \(D_{a,d}\) the six CRPS values added

Reading it: stays in log units · 0 = every daily count exactly right · 0.69 ≈ typically off by 2× · 2.3 ≈ off by 10×. When all 8 futures agree, CRPS is plain \(|x-y|\).

Why subtract half the spread: the first term alone is minimised by collapsing all 8 futures onto one value. Subtracting half the spread makes a forecast shaped like reality the best one; subtracting all of it would let a scattered forecast score below zero. Why log, not percent: percent error is lopsided and caps at 1, so a 10× miss and a 1,000× miss look the same; log keeps counting, which is where the growth accounts live.

Loss 3 — Like-count Poisson (likes, how many)

For every held-out post: how many likes did it get, against the simulation's expected count? Loss 1 checks who liked; this checks how many. Scored once per post at the end of the week.

expected
\( \displaystyle \lambda_q \;=\; \dfrac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} n_{q,k} \qquad \tilde\lambda_q \;=\; \begin{cases} \max\!\big(\lambda_q,\, \tfrac{1}{2K}\big) & y_q > 0 \\ \lambda_q & y_q = 0 \end{cases} \)
per post
\( \displaystyle D_q \;=\; 2\Big(\, y_q \log \dfrac{y_q}{\tilde\lambda_q} \;-\; y_q \;+\; \tilde\lambda_q \Big) \qquad (0 \log 0 = 0) \)
overall
\( \displaystyle L_3 \;=\; \dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G}\; \dfrac{1}{|A_g|}\sum_{a \in A_g}\; \dfrac{1}{|Q_a|}\sum_{q \in Q_a} \dfrac{D_q}{c_a} \)
\(n_{q,k}\)
likes post \(q\) got by the end of the week in future \(k\)
\(y_q\)
likes \(q\) really got by the end of the week (the same count as \(L_q\) in Loss 1)
\(\lambda_q\)
the simulation's expected like count: the average over the 8 futures. Only the average enters this loss; the spread across futures does not
\(\tilde\lambda_q\)
\(\lambda_q\) with a floor of \(\tfrac{1}{16}\) (half a like across 8 futures), used only when the post really got likes. A liked post that got zero likes in every future pays a large but finite price; a correct zero still costs nothing
\(D_q\)
Poisson deviance: twice the gap in Poisson log-likelihood between the simulation's rate and the perfect rate \(y_q\). It is 0 only when \(\lambda_q = y_q\); for a post with no real likes it is \(2\lambda_q\)
\(c_a\)
account \(a\)'s usual likes per post before the week: likes received ÷ posts made (replies count) over Aug 28–Sep 17, 2026, floored at 1; 1 if it made no posts then
\(G,\;A_g,\;Q_a\)
as in Loss 1: groups weigh the same, accounts within a group weigh the same, posts within an account weigh the same

Reading it: 0 = every post's expected count exactly right · for a post at the account's usual level, ≈ 0.61 = predicted 2× too many, ≈ 0.39 = predicted half as many · ≈ 13 = 10× too many, ≈ 2.8 = a tenth as many.

Why Poisson deviance: it is the Poisson log-likelihood shifted so a perfect count scores 0, and its best report is the true expected count, so a simulator cannot gain by shading its counts up or down. It is the count companion to Loss 1: a simulation that gives a post the right number of likes from the wrong people does well here and badly there. Why divide by \(c_a\): deviance grows with the size of the count, so without it one 2,000-like post would outweigh hundreds of 5-like posts. \(c_a\) is fixed before the week starts, so dividing by it changes the scale without changing what the best report is.

The 2-day timescale

Every loss is also scored on 2-day windows of the same 8 futures (the simulation is not restarted) and averaged over the windows. Seven days give six windows, one starting on each of days 1–6, so no day is dropped: \(W_1\) = days 1–2, \(W_2\) = days 2–3, …, \(W_6\) = days 6–7. Days 2–6 fall in two windows, days 1 and 7 in one. The headline likes losses only look at the week's totals; the 2-day versions also check when the likes came. The headline follower loss looks at single days; the 2-day version forgives a follow landing a day early or late but still checks the 2-day totals.

Loss 1, per window
\( \displaystyle S_{q,w} \;=\; \dfrac{1}{\max(L_{q,w},\,1)} \sum_{u \in U_{q,w}} \big(p_{u,q,w} - y_{u,q,w}\big)^2 \)
Loss 1, 2-day
\( \displaystyle L_1^{\text{2d}} \;=\; \dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G}\; \dfrac{1}{|A_g|}\sum_{a \in A_g}\; \dfrac{1}{|W_a|}\sum_{w \in W_a}\; \dfrac{1}{|Q_{a,w}|}\sum_{q \in Q_{a,w}} S_{q,w} \)
Loss 2, per window
\( \displaystyle D_{a,w} \;=\; \sum_{s \in S} \operatorname{CRPS}\big(x^{(s)}_{a,w},\, y^{(s)}_{a,w}\big) \)
Loss 2, 2-day
\( \displaystyle L_2^{\text{2d}} \;=\; \dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G}\; \dfrac{1}{|A_g|}\sum_{a \in A_g}\; \dfrac{1}{6}\sum_{w=1}^{6} D_{a,w} \)
Loss 3, 2-day
\( \displaystyle L_3^{\text{2d}} \;=\; \dfrac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g \in G}\; \dfrac{1}{|A_g|}\sum_{a \in A_g}\; \dfrac{1}{|W_a|}\sum_{w \in W_a} \dfrac{1}{|Q_{a,w}|}\sum_{q \in Q_{a,w}} \dfrac{D_{q,w}}{c_a} \)
\(W_w\)
window \(w\) = days \(w\) and \(w{+}1\), \(w = 1,\dots,6\)
\(Q_{a,w}\)
account \(a\)'s held-out posts made before the end of \(W_w\) (posts that exist during the window, older ones included)
\(W_a\)
the windows in which account \(a\) has at least one such post
likes, 2-day
only likes made inside \(W_w\) count: \(y_{u,q,w}\), \(p_{u,q,w}\), \(U_{q,w}\) and \(L_{q,w}\) are Loss 1's quantities restricted to likes in the window; \(D_{q,w}\) is Loss 3's deviance on the window's real and simulated like counts, same floor, same \(c_a\)
followers, 2-day
each of the six series is summed over the window's two days, then \(\log(1+\cdot)\) and fair CRPS exactly as in Loss 2
empty windows
a post with no likes in a window, real or simulated, scores 0 there and still counts in the average, the same for every simulation

Bars on the leaderboard

Bar height is \(e^{-\text{loss}}\): 1.0 for perfect, 0.5 at a loss of 0.69, 0.25 at 1.39. Entries are ordered by the average of \(e^{-\text{loss}}\) over all six numbers, \(\tfrac{1}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big(e^{-L_i} + e^{-L_i^{\text{2d}}}\big)\), highest first. Entries submitted before Sep 30, 2026 have only \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) at the headline timescale; they are listed after the complete entries, ordered by \(\tfrac{1}{2}\big(e^{-L_1} + e^{-L_2}\big)\).
